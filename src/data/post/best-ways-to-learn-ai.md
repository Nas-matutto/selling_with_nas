## 2. Get Claude to build you a personalized learning plan

**Where to start:** <a href="https://claude.ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">claude.ai</a>
**Cost:** Free plan available, Pro from around $20/month
**Best for:** Anyone who doesn't know what to learn first

The biggest reason people give up on learning AI is that they don't know what to learn next. Generic courses are built for everyone, which means they're built for no one. Claude can fix that by building a plan around your job, your goals, and how much time you actually have.

Copy this prompt into Claude and fill in the brackets:

```
I want to learn AI and get better at using it in my day-to-day work.

About me:
- My job/business: [e.g. I run a Shopify store / I'm a marketing manager]
- My current level: [e.g. I've used ChatGPT a few times but never built anything]
- What I want to be able to do in 30 days: [e.g. automate my customer emails, build a simple website]
- Time I can commit: [e.g. 30 minutes a day, weekdays only]

Build me a 4-week learning plan. For each week, give me:
1. One core concept to understand
2. One hands-on project to build using AI
3. The tools I'll need (free options first)
4. How I'll know I've mastered it

Ask me any questions you need before writing the plan.
```

That last line matters. Claude will come back with a few follow-up questions, and the plan you get afterwards is far more specific to you.

Once you have your plan, keep it inside a Claude Project so every new chat already knows where you are in it. Then use Claude as your tutor along the way: ask it to explain anything you don't understand in simpler terms, or to quiz you at the end of each week. If your plan points you towards building things, my [beginner's guide to Claude Code](/how-to-use-claude-code-for-beginners) is a good next step.

## How to become better at using AI (whichever way you learn)

Learning AI and getting good at using it aren't quite the same thing. Whichever of the methods above you choose, these habits are what turn knowledge into skill:

- **Use it every day, on real work.** Don't save AI for "practice time." Use it for the emails, research, and planning you already have to do.
- **Build something, don't just chat.** Pick one small project, like a landing page, an automation, or a simple tool, and finish it. You'll learn more from one finished project than from a hundred conversations.
- **Give it context.** The more you explain your situation, your goal, and your constraints, the better the output. Most "bad" AI answers come from vague requests.
- **Save the prompts that work.** Keep a simple note of prompts that gave you great results, and reuse them.
- **Teach what you learn.** Explaining a new trick to a colleague or friend is the fastest way to find out whether you really understand it.
